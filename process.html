Design Process

A detailed eight-step design process.

The process is designed to define the project, selections, budget, timeline and expectations before construction begins.

Before Step 1

A design contract—not a construction contract.

The design agreement covers detailed planning that can include existing and proposed layouts, cabinet drawings, engineering when required, scope development, permit-related visits and material selections.

Initial Meeting

The office asks basic questions, then Luke meets at the home for a walkthrough. Clients bring must-haves and wish-list items, discuss possibilities, target investment and a broad cost range. If the project proceeds, a design contract starts detailed planning.

Follow-Up Meeting

Roughly one to two weeks later, the design team returns to laser-measure the home, review scope and begin the preliminary design.

Product Selection & Design

We draft plans and scope while clients select materials such as carpet, flooring, lighting, fixtures, cabinets and countertops, with access to our design studio and local suppliers.

Subcontractor Preview

When appropriate, trade partners visit the home to plan their work, answer questions and offer technical, improvement and cost-saving feedback.

Scope Meeting

Typically two to three weeks after the trade preview, the team reviews the schematic budget, scope updates and product selections, then makes necessary plan changes.

Bidding Process

The estimating team gathers vendor and subcontractor pricing to create a detailed project bid.

Proposal Meeting

The bid is reviewed, questions are answered and any needed project adjustments are discussed.

Start Meeting

If the bid and scope meet expectations, the parties sign the construction agreement, review the timeline and expectations, answer final questions and set the construction start date.
